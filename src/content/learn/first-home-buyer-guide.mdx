Buying your first home involves more moving parts than most people expect going in. This guide walks through the process roughly in the order it comes up.

## 1. Work out your realistic budget

Before you look at a single listing, get a genuine picture of what you can borrow and what you're comfortable repaying   these aren't always the same number. See [how much can I borrow](/articles/how-much-can-i-borrow) for how lenders actually assess this, and use our [borrowing power calculator](/calculators/borrowing-power) for a starting estimate.

## 2. Understand your deposit position

Work out your deposit, then account for the other upfront costs   stamp duty (unless an exemption applies), conveyancing, inspections and loan fees. Our guide on [how much deposit you actually need](/articles/how-much-deposit-do-i-need) covers the LMI trade-off if you're below 20%.

## 3. Check what government support you're eligible for

Depending on your state and circumstances, grants, stamp duty concessions and low-deposit schemes can materially change your numbers. Eligibility rules and benefits vary and change over time, so check current settings rather than assuming.

## 4. Get pre-approved before you inspect seriously

Pre-approval gives you a real, lender-backed budget and stronger negotiating position   see [how pre-approval actually works](/articles/how-does-pre-approval-work) for what's involved.

## 5. Choose fixed, variable, or a split   deliberately

Don't default into whichever rate is advertised loudest. Our guide on [fixed vs variable home loans](/articles/fixed-vs-variable-home-loans) walks through the actual trade-off so you can choose based on your situation, not the headline rate.

## 6. Know what an offset account could do for you

If you'll have savings sitting around after settlement, understanding [what an offset account is](/articles/what-is-an-offset-account) before you choose a loan structure can save meaningful interest over time.

## 7. Make an offer and move to unconditional approval

Once you've found a property, your pre-approval moves toward full approval   the lender orders a valuation and finalises conditions. This is where having compared lenders properly before pre-approval pays off, since switching at this stage is disruptive.

## 8. Settlement day

Funds are transferred, the property title moves to your name, and the keys are yours. From here, your loan structure   offset, extra repayments, fixed/variable   becomes the thing that actually determines how quickly you build equity and how much interest you pay over the life of the loan.

## The single most useful thing you can do early

Talk to a broker before you've locked yourself into a specific lender's process. Comparing your situation across a panel   rather than accepting the first pre-approval you're offered   is the one step most first home buyers skip, and the one most likely to change your outcome.
